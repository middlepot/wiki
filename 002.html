formal observations

tea is a beverage produced by allowing prepared leaves to remain in hot water for a measured period of time. the resulting liquid may vary considerably in colour, fragrance and taste according to the plant, temperature and duration of preparation.

its consumption appears to exceed the ordinary human requirement for hydration. tea is prepared upon waking, during periods of rest, in cold weather, while reading, when receiving visitors, and sometimes when a person is troubled and no immediate solution can be offered.

of particular interest is the custom of preparing tea for another person. the offering is small and nutritionally unremarkable, yet it is frequently understood as an expression of welcome or care.

it therefore seems inaccurate to classify tea solely as a beverage.


field log


entry 002 — the custom of sharing warmth

“ i prepared tea this afternoon according to the usual procedure : leaves, hot water, and several minutes of waiting.

the waiting appears important.

i sat beside the window while it steeped and noticed how the cup gradually warmed my hands. there was rain outside, though the room itself was dry and sufficiently warm. by all practical measures, the tea was unnecessary. still, i did not hurry.

humans seem to make tea when they wish to place a small boundary between one part of the day and another. they sit down. they hold something warm. sometimes they prepare a second cup simply because another person is nearby.

i had previously classified this behaviour as the preparation of a beverage, but i think it may also be a way of saying : stay here with me for a little while. ”
