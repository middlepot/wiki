handwritten letters


words given a body, so they may be held.

formal observations

a letter is a form of communication in which words are manually inscribed upon paper and entrusted to another person. unlike spoken conversation, the message may travel independently of its writer and remain intact long after the exchange has ended.

the method is notably inefficient. writing requires time, delivery introduces delay, errors cannot always be removed without leaving evidence behind. nevertheless, humans continue to regard handwritten correspondence with unusual tenderness.

of particular interest is the preservation of letters after their practical purpose has expired. they are folded again along familiar creases, tied together, placed inside drawers and boxes, and sometimes kept for entire lifetimes.

perhaps this is because handwriting records more than language. the movement of a particular hand remains upon the page.

a letter may therefore be classified not only as communication, but as a small physical trace of someone who was once there.


field log


entry 003 — words that remain

“ a few days ago, i received a letter, and examined it for longer than was necessary.

the handwriting was not perfectly even. one word had been crossed out, and near the bottom of the page the ink became slightly fainter. these irregularities should have made the message less precise. instead, they seemed to make the person who wrote it feel strangely near. i read it twice.

afterward, i placed it again carefully inside its envelope. there was no practical reason to keep it ; i had already understood everything it said.

still, throwing it away felt impossible.

i think i understand why humans preserve letters. words spoken aloud disappear almost as soon as they are heard, but these ones remain where a hand once placed them.

it is comforting to have proof that someone thought of you while you were apart. ”
