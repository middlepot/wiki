bubble baths


a little sea of warmth and fleeting clouds, where the day's troubles are permitted to dissolve.

formal observations

a bubble bath is a form of bathing in which warm water is combined with a foaming preparation, producing numerous small, air-filled spheres upon the surface. these bubbles are temporary structures, gradually disappearing through evaporation, drainage and contact with surrounding surfaces.

although bathing serves the practical purpose of cleansing the body, the addition of bubbles appears to be largely recreational. humans deliberately introduce fragrance, warmth and other pleasant sensations into an activity that could otherwise be completed much more efficiently.

of particular interest is the tendency to remain immersed after the cleansing process has concluded. this behaviour suggests that the bath serves a secondary purpose, possibly related to emotional regulation and the temporary withdrawal from everyday responsibilities.

the bubbles themselves are especially curious : despite their fragility and brief existence, humans seem to find considerable pleasure in their presence, meaning that they are not required to last in order to provide comfort.


field log


entry 004 — an inquiry into little clouds

“ i conducted an experiment last evening involving warm water, a fragrant preparation and a rather excessive quantity of bubbles.

the resulting structures were remarkably delicate. i attempted to hold one between my fingers, but it disappeared almost immediately. after several unsuccessful trials, i concluded that they are better admired than collected.

i remained in the water for some time, observing the little clouds drifting around me. the room was quiet, and the warmth seemed to soften thoughts i had not realised were troubling me.

eventually, the bubbles began to disappear. i felt a peculiar reluctance to leave.

i had previously assumed that bathing was simply a method of cleansing the body, but i now suspect humans have discovered another purpose for it : a temporary little world in which nothing is expected of them.

i would like to keep visiting this world. ”
